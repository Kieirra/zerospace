This guide is for players who already know RTS games, or who come from the campaign or co-op, and want to start playing Versus. It follows the order of a game, from the choices you make before it starts to the replays you watch once it is over.

## Before the game

Before the game you choose a faction and one of the 6 mercenaries. The mercenary adds its own hero, units, buildings, talents and topbar ability to your faction, so the same faction can play quite differently from one game to the next.

![The 4 factions and the 6 mercenaries](/images/guides/getting-started-versus/factions-and-mercenaries.webp)

Each faction has its own way of playing.

| Faction | Playstyle |
|---|---|
| [The Legion](/wiki/the-legion/units) | The only faction that can have its 3 heroes on the field at the same time, with spectacular effects. It feels close to the Night Elves in Warcraft III. |
| [Grell](/wiki/grell/units) | The closest to the Zerg in StarCraft, with large numbers of units and creep. |
| [Protectorate](/wiki/protectorate/units) | The classic faction, with a tool for almost every situation, but it asks for more game knowledge. |
| [The Xol](/wiki/the-xol/units) | The newest faction, built around duplicating units and merging them. |

## During the game

### Economy

Your starting base sits on a rich hexite field and gives 504 hexite per minute, while a standard expansion gives 336 hexite. Expanding is still useful, but less than in other RTS games. This is why many builds make units first and take their second base a bit later.

Be careful with flux harvesters. Their number is capped, and when one dies it comes back for free. You can never go below the number you have built, so think before you add one.

### XP towers and talents

Experience is what really sets ZeroSpace apart from other RTS games. XP unlocks talents, which are passive or active abilities, and they are strong enough to decide a game.

![A hero walking to an XP tower guarded by creeps, with the capture circle around it](/images/guides/getting-started-versus/xp-tower.webp)

Most maps have 3 XP towers, and only one map has 4. To take a tower, you kill the creeps that guard it and then stay close to it. In the early game your heroes usually do this job. Later on, a single unit is often enough to take back a tower that the opponent has captured.

You also gain XP slowly over time, and some talents give more. For example, [War Experience](/wiki/the-legion/talent/war-experience) gives The Legion XP for each enemy unit it destroys, and [Life Path](/wiki/marran/talent/life-path) from Marran gives 25% more XP.

There are 7 talent levels, and at each level you choose one of two talents. On levels 2, 4 and 6, one of the two choices comes from your mercenary.

![The Legion talents, with two talents per level from LV1 to LV7 and the mercenary talent on LV2, LV4 and LV6](/images/guides/getting-started-versus/talents.webp)

### Buildings block shots

Buildings block shots, so units that stand behind them are protected. This gives the defender a strong advantage. Many new players run into a base, lose the fight and do not understand why, and the buildings are usually the reason. Several patches have reduced this advantage, especially for buildings placed in the middle of the map, but it is still there.

![Units on each side of a building, which stands between them and blocks the shots](/images/guides/getting-started-versus/buildings-block-shots.webp)

So do not attack a base too early. Most buildings cost only 50 to 100 hexite and take a long time to destroy, which makes the trade rarely worth it. Wait until your army can kill buildings quickly, usually in the mid or late game. Units that deal big hits do much better here than units that deal many small ones, and some units are made for this job, like the [Mammoth](/wiki/the-legion/unit/mammoth) with 150% bonus damage against buildings. The same idea works against heavily armoured units.

In the early game it pays more to choke your opponent. Keep them from leaving their base, and hunt the extractors on their bases and the harvesters on their flux.

### High ground

Units on high ground deal 15% more damage to units below them. On top of that, units below have no vision of the high ground, so they cannot fire back even while they take damage. Only air units can reach them, or units that walk up the ramp, which is rarely a good idea.

## After the game

The [guides](/guides) on this site give you build orders to follow step by step. To see how the best players play, the best way is to watch their replays.

1. Open the [1v1 leaderboard](https://playzerospace.com/rankings?board=Ldr_MMR_1v1), click a player and download one of their replays.
2. In the game, open Versus (1) and then Replays (2). Select any replay (3) and click Browse to file (4), which opens the replays folder.
3. Put the downloaded replay in that folder, and it will show up in the Replays list.

[![The Versus menu with the Replays tab, a selected replay and the Browse to file button](/images/guides/getting-started-versus/replays.webp)](/images/guides/getting-started-versus/replays.webp)

If you have questions, the community is happy to help on the official [ZeroSpace Discord](https://discord.gg/zerospace).
